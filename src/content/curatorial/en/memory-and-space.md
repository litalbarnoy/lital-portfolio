## The Idea

The exhibition began with a simple question: what remains of a place after it changes completely? The curation combined archival objects, oral testimonies from longtime residents, and work by three contemporary artists responding to the archival material.

## The Process

Two months of work in the museum archive, sorting through hundreds of photographs and documents, alongside recorded interviews with residents who lived in the area before a major urban transformation took place. The main curatorial challenge was balancing respect for the historical material with interpretive space for the artists.

## The Result

The show was built as a reverse chronological path — starting in the present and moving backward in time, so visitors encounter the outcome first and only then the story that led to it.

## Reception

The resident testimonies gathered for the exhibition were later transferred to the museum's permanent archive, a lasting contribution beyond the run of the show itself.
